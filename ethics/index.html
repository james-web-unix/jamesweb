Ethics & Responsible Use Policy

James Web teaches security for learning and defence. Every lesson assumes one rule: you test only systems you own or have explicit, written authorization to test.

Written by AR. Abhinav Ranjan · Last reviewed 3 October 2026

The permission-first model

Technical skill is not enough. Before any hands-on work you need to know what you are authorized to test, and when you must stop. Every James Web lesson, example and exercise assumes this order:

Permission→Scope→Safe testing→Evidence→Reporting

The Legal Lab Checklist

Run these five checks before every practice session. If you cannot complete all five, do not start the session.

  1. Confirm permission and scope. Make sure you have explicit authorization for the specific target and testing activity.
  2. Set target boundaries. Write down exactly which systems, IP addresses or domains are in scope.
  3. Define the session goal. State what you are trying to learn or verify before you begin.
  4. Set stop conditions. Decide in advance when testing must stop.
  5. Keep evidence notes. Record what you tested, what happened and what still needs verification.

What James Web publishes, and what it does not

We publish concepts, defensive thinking, safe lab setups and practice on systems you own or are allowed to test.

We do not publish material intended to gain unauthorized access to a real person’s or organization’s systems, instructions aimed at named third parties, or guidance on avoiding detection by the owner or by law enforcement.

Where to practise safely

  • Virtual machines and home labs that you own and control.
  • Capture-the-flag (CTF) platforms and training labs that invite testing.
  • Bug bounty or disclosure programs, only within their published scope and rules.

The law

Unauthorized access to computer systems is a crime in most countries. Examples include India’s Information Technology Act, 2000, the US Computer Fraud and Abuse Act and the UK Computer Misuse Act 1990. Permission must be explicit and cover the specific target and activity. This is general information and not legal advice.

If you find a problem

If you stumble on a weakness in a system you do not own, stop, do not access or copy data, and tell the owner through their official channel.

To report a security issue on this website, use the Contact page; the site’s security.txt file lists the current reporting channel. Please test only this site’s public pages, avoid disrupting visitors, and give us reasonable time to respond. For factual mistakes in the books or pages, see Corrections.
